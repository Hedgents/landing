# Tokenized Silver Is Far Behind Tokenized Gold. Here Is Why, and Why It Matters

*On-chain gold is a multi-billion-dollar market. On-chain silver is a rounding error next to it. The gap is not an accident, and it is not permanent.*

By **Hedgents Research**

---

**TL;DR.** As of June 2026, tokenized gold is roughly a **$5.1B** on-chain market. Tokenized silver is roughly **$250M**, about one twentieth the size, and a single token (Kinesis Silver) accounts for nearly all of it. The gap is structural: gold is the monetary metal and the reflex safe-haven trade, while silver is half-industrial and a messier story to underwrite. Silver also costs more to vault per dollar and has thinner standalone token demand. None of that means an allocator should accept gold-only exposure on-chain. The current gold/silver ratio sits near **63**, below its long-run average, and silver is running its sixth straight supply deficit. Silver belongs in a real multi-metal allocation today, not as a teaser for later. That is part of the case for a managed four-metal index rather than a stack of single-metal longs.

## The size gap, in numbers

Start with the scoreboard, because it is lopsided in a way that surprises people.

Tokenized gold is a real market. The CoinGecko tokenized-gold category sits near **$5.1B** in mid-June 2026, and tokenized-gold spot trading hit **$90.7B in Q1 2026** alone, already past the full-year 2025 total. Two issuers dominate: Tether Gold (XAUT, near $2.7B) and Paxos Gold (PAXG, near $2.2B), together more than 90% of the category.

Tokenized silver is not in the same weight class. The CoinGecko tokenized-silver category is around **$250M** as of June 2026. One token, **Kinesis Silver (KAG)**, is roughly **$248M** of that, backed one-to-one by allocated bullion in audited vaults. Everything else is small. **Matrixdock Silver (XAGm)**, the LBMA Good Delivery silver token that launched in March 2026, is only a few million in market cap so far. A handful of others (tokenized iShares Silver exposure via Ondo, Remora's SLVr ETF wrapper, a few regional tokens) are smaller still.

So the ratio of on-chain markets is roughly **20 to 1** in gold's favor. In the underlying metal, gold is more valuable per ounce, but the world holds far more silver tonnage above ground, and physical silver investment demand is large and growing. The on-chain gap is not a reflection of the physical market. It is a reflection of who has bothered to build, and who has bothered to buy.

It is worth saying plainly that these issuers are not adversaries here. Kinesis, Matrixdock, Ondo, and the rest are the **supply rail**: the regulated, custodied, attested silver inventory that any index or hedged product has to source from. The thinness of that rail is the problem to describe, not a competitor to beat.

## Why gold ran ahead: four structural reasons

The gap is not random. Four things push gold to the front of the tokenization queue and leave silver behind.

**1. Gold is the monetary metal. Silver is half-industrial.** Gold's entire investment thesis is one clean sentence: it is the reflex safe-haven asset, the thing capital runs to when it distrusts everything else. A tokenized gold product inherits that one-line pitch intact. Silver's story is split. Industrial and technology fabrication (solar PV, electronics, automotive, and a growing data-center and AI component) accounted for roughly **60%** of total silver demand in 2025, per the Silver Institute's World Silver Survey 2026, even as 2026 industrial offtake is forecast to dip about 2% on high prices. That makes silver a hybrid: part monetary hedge, part cyclical industrial input. A hybrid is harder to market to a treasury that just wants "digital gold," and it trades with more volatility, which makes the token harder to position.

**2. The default crypto-to-metal trade is gold.** When a DAO treasury or a crypto-native fund decides to add real-metal exposure, the first and often only instinct is gold. It is the familiar door. Silver is the second thought, and second thoughts get smaller allocations and fewer dedicated products. Issuers build where the demand already is, which deepens gold's lead and starves silver of the liquidity that would attract more issuers. It is a self-reinforcing loop.

**3. Vaulting economics cut against silver.** Silver is bulky. A given dollar value of silver occupies far more vault space than the same value in gold, roughly two ounces of gold versus more than a thousand ounces of silver for the same six-figure sum. That shows up directly in storage fees: it is common to see allocated silver vaulting priced around **0.30%** a year against roughly **0.12%** for gold, a 2.5x difference on a percentage basis. For a tokenized product that has to pass custody cost through to the holder (often as a slowly declining ounces-per-token figure), silver is simply a more expensive metal to hold on-chain. Thinner margins, thinner incentive to launch.

**4. Standalone silver-token demand is thin.** Even where silver tokens exist, the demand for a pure single-metal silver position on-chain has been modest. Kinesis aside, most silver tokens trade tiny volumes. A buyer who wants silver exposure has historically reached for a familiar ETF (SLV and its peers) or physical bullion, not an SPL or ERC-20 silver token. Without a clear on-chain use (collateral, composability, a basket to slot into), the standalone silver token has struggled to find its buyer. Supply has shown up faster than demand.

## The gold/silver ratio, and why silver is not a sideshow

Here is the part that matters for an allocator. The reasons silver trails on-chain are about plumbing and marketing. They are not a verdict on silver as an asset.

The **gold/silver ratio**, how many ounces of silver it takes to buy one ounce of gold, sat near **63** in late June 2026 (roughly $4,200 gold against $66 silver on June 22). That is below the 50-year average of about 65 to 70. The ratio has been volatile this cycle: it spiked into the 80s during the spring correction and touched the low 50s in late January when silver hit its all-time high. A ratio drifting below the long-run average has historically marked silver's outperformance phases, the windows when silver does the catching up.

The supply backdrop reinforces it. Silver is on track for its **sixth consecutive annual market deficit** in 2026, per the Silver Institute, with above-ground stocks drawn down materially since 2021. Industrial demand softens at the margin on slower solar offtake, but data-center, AI, and automotive uses are picking up the slack, and investment demand is forecast to stay strong. None of that is a price call. It is just the reason silver is a legitimate, independent metal allocation and not a junior appendage to gold.

Which is the whole point. An investor who wants real metals diversification should not be quietly funneled into a gold-only position simply because gold is the only metal with deep on-chain supply. A portfolio that is 100% tokenized gold is not "diversified into metals." It is concentrated in one metal that happens to be the easiest one to tokenize. That is a plumbing decision masquerading as an allocation decision.

## What a real multi-metal basket needs from silver

If you are building actual metals exposure, silver is core, not a future feature. A basket that holds gold, silver, platinum, and palladium captures distinct drivers: gold's monetary-hedge behavior, silver's hybrid monetary-plus-industrial profile, and the platinum-group metals' tighter industrial and automotive links. Those four do not move in lockstep, and that is exactly why you hold more than one. The gold/silver ratio swinging from the low 50s to the 80s inside six months is not noise to a basket. It is the rebalancing opportunity that a single-metal long cannot touch.

The catch is that the on-chain silver rail is thin and concentrated. One token holds nearly all the tokenized-silver market cap. A passive buyer who simply wants to "add silver on-chain" inherits that concentration: one issuer's custody, one issuer's redemption terms, one token's liquidity depth setting their exit. That is precisely the single-issuer risk that good metals allocation is supposed to spread.

This is the argument for active management over passive aggregation. A managed basket can source silver from whatever rail is deepest and most substitutable at the moment, weight it deliberately against the other three metals, and rebalance into the ratio swings instead of riding them passively. The thinness of on-chain silver is not a reason to skip silver. It is a reason to manage the exposure rather than buy a single token and hope.
